This guide translates Neville Goddard's Revision technique into a practical framework for day traders. The core idea:
your mental state, emotional reactions, and internal narrative directly shape your future trading performance. By
revising the events of each trading day in your imagination — rewriting mistakes as successes — you reprogram the
subconscious patterns that drive impulsive, emotional decisions.
1. THE CORE PRINCIPLE
Goddard taught that the mind is like a garden. Whatever you plant — through your thoughts, feelings, and replays of
events — will grow and manifest in your outer reality. For traders, this is literal: the mental movies you run after a bad
trade become the blueprint for your next one.
When you replay a blown stop-loss or a missed entry with anger and self-criticism, you are watering weeds. You are
wiring your nervous system to repeat that pattern. Revision is the pruning shears — the tool to cut those weeds before
they take root.
 "The day is not slipping into the past. It is always advancing into the future to confront you, either pruned
or in some strange weed-like state."
2. THE 7 DEADLY TRADER MISTAKES & THE REVISION FIX
Below are the most common psychological mistakes traders make, and exactly how to use Revision to reprogram each
one:

✗ REVENGE TRADING
After a loss, you jump back in immediately — bigger size, no plan — trying to 'win it back.' You are trading from
wounded ego, not strategy. The market doesn't owe you anything.
■ REVISION FIX:
That evening, close your eyes. See that losing trade. Now revise the scene: you took the loss, nodded calmly, closed
your laptop, and walked away with discipline. Feel the pride of that restraint as vividly as possible. Replay it 3–5x until it
feels real. Tomorrow your nervous system will have a new memory to draw from.
✗ MOVING YOUR STOP LOSS
Price approaches your stop and instead of respecting your plan, you widen it — 'just a little more room.' This turns a
small loss into a catastrophic one and destroys risk management.
■ REVISION FIX:
Revise the moment: you watched price hit your stop, the trade closed automatically, and you felt relieved — grateful the
system protected you. Replay that feeling of trust in your rules repeatedly.
✗ FOMO — CHASING ENTRIES
You miss the setup, watch price move without you, then chase it at a terrible R:R. You entered from fear of missing out,
not from edge.
■ REVISION FIX:
Revise the scene: you saw the move, felt the pull, and simply said 'the next setup.' You waited. Another opportunity
came. Imagine the calm confidence of a patient sniper who only takes the perfect shot.
✗ OVERTRADING
You've hit your daily goal but keep trading out of boredom, excitement, or ego. You give back profits and end red. More
trades does not equal more profit.
■ REVISION FIX:
Revise today's end of session: you hit your target, shut down the platform, and did something enjoyable. Feel the
satisfaction of walking away a winner. That image — you as a disciplined professional — is the identity you're building.
✗ IGNORING THE PLAN
You had a solid trade plan. Then in the moment, emotions took over — you took a random trade, sized up recklessly,
or ignored key levels. Execution failed.
■ REVISION FIX:
Revise each deviation: rewind the session and replay every trade as if you executed perfectly according to your plan.
See yourself pulling the trigger at the right entry, right size, right stop. Feel the alignment of thought, plan, and action.

✗ NEGATIVE SELF-TALK AFTER LOSSES
'I'm a terrible trader.' 'I always do this.' 'I'll never be consistent.' This inner dialogue is not honesty — it is programming.
What you repeatedly say about yourself becomes your trading identity.
■ REVISION FIX:
This is Revision at its most powerful. After a hard day, consciously rewrite the internal script: 'I learn fast. I adjust. Every
loss is data.' Goddard: forgiveness means identifying yourself with the ideal you want to become — not condemning
what you were.
✗ EGO — THE NEED TO BE RIGHT
You refuse to cut a losing trade because exiting means admitting you were wrong. Your ego is more invested in being
right than in making money. Pros trade P&L; amateurs trade ego.
■ REVISION FIX:
Revise the moment: see yourself closing the losing position without hesitation, then journaling 'I was wrong and I
managed it perfectly.' Imagine a version of yourself who finds joy in tight risk management — who sees small losses as
the cost of doing business, not a reflection of worth.
3. THE DAILY REVISION PROTOCOL
Goddard's exact method, adapted for traders. Do this every evening after markets close — 10–15 minutes that
compound like interest:
STEP 1 — REVIEW WITHOUT JUDGMENT
Scroll through your trade log or journal. Watch the replay. Don't criticize — observe. You are the scientist reviewing
data, not the defendant on trial.
STEP 2 — IDENTIFY THE WEEDS
Mark every moment where emotion overrode plan: the revenge trade, the chased entry, the held loser. These are the
scenes you will revise.
STEP 3 — REWRITE THE SCRIPT
In your imagination, replay each scene — but this time you execute perfectly. You take the stop. You wait for the setup.
You close the platform after your target. Make it vivid. Add emotion: feel the calm, the confidence, the pride.
STEP 4 — REPLAY UNTIL IT FEELS REAL
Goddard said to repeat the revised scene until it 'takes on the tones of reality.' Your brain cannot fully distinguish a
vividly imagined event from a real one — this is the mechanism.
STEP 5 — FALL ASLEEP IN THE REVISED STATE

4. THE ELITE MINDSET SHIFT
Goddard said that in the early stages of using Revision, you will see tangible results — increased income, better
outcomes. But the deeper transformation is in who you become. For traders, that means:
◆ You stop reacting and start responding — there is a gap between stimulus and action.
◆ Losses become data, not identity attacks.
◆ You trade the plan, not the emotion.
◆ You develop deep trust in your edge because you've mentally rehearsed executing it hundreds of times.
◆ You naturally attract better setups because your pattern recognition is no longer clouded by fear and ego.
